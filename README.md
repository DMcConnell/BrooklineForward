# #BrooklineForward — Project Repository

**Mission:** Build a rigorous, data-backed citizen proposal to solve Brookline's structural budget deficit — not through another override cycle, but through permanent fiscal reform.

**Status:** Active research phase  |  Started March 2026  |  Based on E&RSC Final Report (March 13, 2026)

---

## The Problem in One Paragraph

Brookline's revenue is legally capped at ~3%/year growth (Proposition 2½). Its expenses — healthcare benefits, debt service, union wages, special education — grow at 5–8%/year. The gap compounds every year. Brookline has held four operating overrides since 2008, each one larger and closer together than the last. The E&RSC projects a $22M structural deficit by FY29. This project proposes a path out that doesn't rely on a fifth, sixth, and seventh override.

## The Three-Branch Proposal

Brookline's structural deficit requires structural solutions. The proposal operates on three mutually reinforcing tracks:

### Branch 1 — Economic Development
**The insight:** Commercial property is taxed at up to 175% of the residential rate and generates tax revenue without adding school-age children. Brookline has dramatically underperformed its commercial development potential for decades.

**Key workstreams:**
- **Site inventory** — Map and prioritize the top 10–15 commercial development opportunities
- **Zoning reform** — Identify specific changes needed to unlock stalled sites; learn from Cambridge/Somerville as-of-right models
- **PILOT program** — Structured payments-in-lieu-of-taxes from Brookline's large non-profit landholders
- **Asset monetization** — Parking, golf course, school facilities off-hours
- **Out-of-district SpEd expansion** — Brookline's specialized programs (Winthrop House, ALC, LAHB) could generate tuition revenue from neighboring districts

**Headline opportunity:** CHCA alone (~7yr timeline) = ~$5.6M/year. A systematic strategy across all sites could be transformational.

**10-year revenue potential:** $8–15M/year at full build-out

---

### Branch 2 — Legislative Strategy
**The insight:** When non-resident students (METCO + Materials Fee) require special education, Brookline bears the full cost — ~$28,000/student/year — with zero reimbursement from the student's home district or the State. With 144 non-resident SpEd students, this is ~$4M/year Brookline subsidizes for other municipalities. Newton, Wellesley, and Lexington face the identical problem. Together, these communities have a compelling case at the State House.

**Key workstreams:**
- **METCO SpEd reimbursement** — Quantify the cost, build the coalition, find the legislative champion
- **Chapter 70 reform** — Analyze whether Brookline is underfunded relative to its foundation budget
- **New revenue tools** — Real estate transfer tax, Home Rule petition options, Prop 2½ modernization
- **Legislative mapping** — State House delegation contacts, active bills, best legislative vehicle

**Near-term opportunity:** $4–8M/year in recovered costs if legislation passes

---

### Branch 3 — 2030 OPEB Pivot
**The insight:** In 2030, Brookline fully funds its pension liability — a decade ahead of the state mandate. This frees ~$40M/year currently flowing to pension contributions. The Select Board's current policy is to redirect all of it to OPEB funding. The E&RSC's own OPEB section questioned this. No one has built a rigorous model of the alternatives.

**Key workstreams:**
- **Liability baseline** — Extract key figures from the GASB 74/75 actuarial report
- **Scenario modeling** — Four scenarios for how to allocate the $40M (all-OPEB / split / dynamic / healthcare-first)
- **Moody's sensitivity** — Which OPEB funding level preserves the Aaa rating?
- **Healthcare reform** — Model savings from reducing the 83% employer premium share; GIC option
- **Comparables** — How have Newton, Wellesley, Lexington planned for this transition?

**Structural opportunity:** A well-designed OPEB pivot could free $10–20M/year for operating relief starting FY31

## Working Principles

1. **Ground everything in data.** Every claim needs a source. Every revenue estimate needs a methodology.
2. **Be honest about uncertainty.** Ranges with assumptions are better than false precision.
3. **Respect what the E&RSC got right.** We're building on their work, not replacing it.
4. **Think structurally.** One-time fixes don't solve compounding problems.
5. **Make it actionable.** The final proposal needs to be something the Select Board and Town Meeting can actually act on.
