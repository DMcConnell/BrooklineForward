Brookline Budget Explorer

Interactive charts built from official Town budget documents. See where the money comes from, where it goes, and why the gap between revenue and costs keeps growing.

FY2021 – FY2030 Sources: FY25 Financial Plan, FY26–30 LRFP, Article 9, ERSC Report

Revenue vs. Expenditures

The core structural challenge: costs grow faster than revenue, opening a widening gap.

Revenue Expenditures Projected
Key insight: Revenue grows ~2–3% per year while expenditures grow ~4–6%. By FY2030, the cumulative gap reaches $31.7 million — roughly 7% of operating revenue.

Cumulative Projected Deficit

Without structural changes, the gap between revenue and costs compounds year after year.

Key insight: The School deficit (~$20.8M by FY30) is roughly twice the Town-side deficit (~$10.9M), reflecting faster growth in education costs relative to the 60/40 funding split.

Revenue by Source

Property taxes dominate Brookline's revenue — and are capped by Proposition 2 1/2.

Key insight: Property tax provides ~72% of all revenue but grows only ~3%/year under Prop 2 1/2. Free Cash usage spiked in FY24-25 to backfill the gap — an unsustainable pattern.

Expenditures by Category

Education and benefits together consume over 73% of the budget — and both grow faster than revenue.

Key insight: Schools and benefits alone account for $292M in FY30 — up from $225M in FY25. Debt service nearly doubles from $37M to $54M due to school construction borrowing.

Benefits Cost Drivers

Health insurance and pensions are the fastest-growing costs in the budget.

Key insight: Group health insurance grows from $38M to $61M (+60%) in just 5 years. The pension burden drops in FY30 as Brookline reaches full funding — a major transition point.

Department Spending Trends

Six-year spending trajectory for major town departments and schools.

Key insight: Police spending jumped 9.8% in FY26 due to a new contract with 18% total pay increases. Public Works rose 9.3% partly due to solid waste privatization costs.

Where Every Dollar Goes (FY2025)

The fully-allocated general fund operating budget by function.

Key insight: Nearly 60 cents of every dollar goes to education. Combined with non-school benefits (14%) and public safety (11.8%), these three areas consume 85% of the budget.

The Salary Step Escalator

Nominal COLA is 2.75%, but step increases push actual educator raises to 5–8% — well above area inflation of 3.2%.

Key insight: About 52% of Brookline educators are on the step grid. A first-year teacher with a Bachelor's gets an 8.0% raise — nearly 3x the nominal COLA. This compounds across hundreds of positions every year. Source: ERSC Report, p. 64.

Enrollment Falls, Cost Per Student Rises

School enrollment is declining toward a projected trough — but the budget keeps growing.

Key insight: Enrollment dropped from 7,762 (SY19-20) to a projected low of 6,886 (SY27-28) — down 11%. Meanwhile, the school budget grew from $121M to $142M. Cost per student rose from $15,557 to $20,539 — up 32%. Source: ERSC Report (Cropper-McKibben forecast).

Staffing Shifts: FY2019 → FY2026

Where the town added and cut positions over seven years. Total: +18.8 FTEs, +$13.1M in salary.

Key insight: Police lost 7.5 FTE positions while Health gained 8.24. Total town salary budget grew 24.6% ($53.2M → $66.3M) — well above inflation. Source: ERSC Report, p. 108.

What's Driving Budget Growth

Four categories account for the bulk of budget growth from FY2019 to FY2026.

Key insight: Debt service grew at 20.1% annually — by far the fastest. This is driven by school construction borrowing (High School, Driscoll, Pierce, fire stations). Pensions grew at 8.1%, health insurance at 5.1%. Source: ERSC Report, p. 107.

The Non-Resident SpEd Subsidy

Brookline educates non-resident students who are significantly more likely to need — and receive — special education services, at a cost the town largely absorbs.

Key insight: METCO students receive SpEd at 2.2x the rate of residents (34.8% vs 15.9%). The unreimbursed cost is ~$4M/year — roughly the size of the FY26 Town-side deficit. Brookline also receives below-average METCO funding ($6,670/student vs. state average $8,026). Source: ERSC Report, pp. 68-72.

BEEP: Tuition vs. True Cost

Brookline's early education program charges families only a fraction of what it actually costs to run.

Key insight: BEEP's true cost is $42,832 per student (101.4 FTE staff for 253 students). Tuition covers only 63% of the breakeven cost — and that breakeven figure excludes overhead allocated elsewhere. The total program costs $10.8M/year. Source: ERSC Report, pp. 79-87.

About this data

All figures come from official Town of Brookline documents: the FY2025 Financial Plan, the FY2026–FY2030 Long Range Financial Plan (December 2024), the Article 9 FY2026 budget motion approved by the Advisory Committee (15-0-0) in May 2025, and the Expenditures & Revenues Study Committee (ERSC) Final Report (March 2026).

FY2021–FY2024 figures are actuals. FY2025–FY2026 are adopted budgets. FY2027–FY2030 are projections from the LRFP and carry the assumptions noted in that document (12% health insurance increase in FY26, 10% thereafter; 2.5% state aid growth; pension full-funding by 2030).
