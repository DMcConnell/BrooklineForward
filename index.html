Understanding the Challenge

Brookline's finances face
a real structural challenge.

Brookline's property taxes — 79% of all operating revenue — grow at around 3% per year under Proposition 2½, Massachusetts' 1980 tax limitation law. Meanwhile, healthcare premiums, wages, debt service from school construction, and special education costs tend to grow faster — often 5–8% annually.

Over time, that gap compounds. The town's own Expenditures & Revenues Study Committee — a year-long, rigorous, independent review — published its findings in March 2026 and did serious, honest work identifying this dynamic and what's driving it.

BrooklineForward builds on that foundation. We're researching the long-term structural levers the town has available — on the revenue side, the legislative side, and in how it manages major upcoming financial transitions. We think there are real, credible paths forward, and we're trying to map them carefully.

Three Areas We're Exploring

Our research is organized
around three connected tracks.

The E&RSC's report is an excellent foundation. It also explicitly noted areas it didn't have time to fully explore — economic development, state legislative options, and the major financial transition coming in 2030. Those are the areas we're digging into, starting with the data and working toward recommendations. Nothing here is settled — this is active research and we welcome pushback.

🏗️

Branch 1

Economic Development

Commercial property generates tax revenue at a higher rate than residential, without adding school-age children. Brookline has significant untapped potential here — from new development to better use of existing assets to structured partnerships with the town's large non-profit landholders. We're trying to quantify what's actually realistic and on what timeline.

10-year potential $8–15M / yr
  • Commercial parcel inventory & fiscal impact modeling
  • Zoning reform for stalled high-value sites
  • PILOT program for non-profit landholders
  • Out-of-district SpEd revenue expansion
🏛️

Branch 2

State & Legislative Options

Several aspects of Brookline's fiscal picture are shaped by state law and state funding formulas. We're researching what options exist at the legislative level — including how special education costs for non-resident students are handled, how Chapter 70 education aid is calculated, and what local option revenue tools other Massachusetts communities have pursued.

Recovered costs potential $4–8M / yr
  • METCO SpEd unreimbursed cost quantification
  • Coalition building with Newton, Wellesley, Lexington
  • State House delegation engagement
  • Chapter 70, Prop 2½, and transfer tax options
📊

Branch 3

The 2030 Financial Transition

In 2030, Brookline is on track to fully fund its pension liability — a genuine fiscal achievement. This frees up a significant annual cash flow that the town will need to allocate thoughtfully. The E&RSC flagged this as an important open question. We're modeling the options carefully, including what different choices would mean for Brookline's long-term financial health and credit rating.

Operating relief potential (FY31+) $10–20M / yr
  • GASB 74/75 actuarial liability baseline
  • Four $40M allocation scenarios modeled
  • Moody's Aaa rating sensitivity analysis
  • GIC healthcare reform savings model

Built on Official Work

We start with Brookline's
own rigorous research.

The Town's Expenditures & Revenues Study Committee spent a full year producing a 162-page independent report published in March 2026. It's thorough, honest work — and it's the foundation for everything we do here. We're not trying to relitigate their findings; we're trying to extend them.

The committee itself noted areas it didn't have the mandate or time to fully explore: long-term economic development strategy, state legislative options, and how to handle the major financial transition coming in 2030. That's where BrooklineForward picks up.

The E&RSC also identified nine specific structural reforms they're calling for from the Schools — these form an important part of the picture we're working from.

The E&RSC's 9 structural reform conditions for the Schools

1
Negotiate healthcare premium share down from 83% (comparable median: ~75–78%)
2
Create mechanism to manage wage expense growth toward revenue growth
3
Commission a middle school model and classroom consolidation study
4
Complete MUNIS shared financial system implementation by end of FY27
5
Verify all teaching assignments comply with contractual terms
6
Audit and reduce technology leases and ed-tech subscriptions
7
Require third-party school space users (7am–5:30pm) to pay fair-market rent
8
Launch facilities governance and youth athletics coordination pilot in FY27
9
Move the BEEP early education revolving fund toward full-cost break-even
