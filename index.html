The Structural Deficit

This isn't a discipline problem.
It's a math problem.

Brookline's property taxes — 79% of all operating revenue — are legally capped at 2.5% growth per year under Proposition 2½, Massachusetts' 1980 tax limitation law. In practice, the levy grows about 3% annually in real terms.

Expenses don't have a cap. Healthcare premiums, union wages, debt service from school construction, and special education costs all grow at 5–8% per year. The gap compounds every year. The only legal remedies are: cut services, raise fees, grow the tax base, or ask voters for another override.

The Town has done honest work. The E&RSC's 162-page report confirms real, painful cuts have already been made by both Town and Schools. This isn't about waste. It's about a structural mismatch — and it requires a structural fix.

The Three-Branch Proposal

A structural fix, not another override cycle.

The E&RSC did serious, honest work — and explicitly identified areas it couldn't fully explore, including economic development strategy, state legislative options, and the 2030 pension windfall decision. Those gaps are where this proposal lives.

🏗️

Branch 1

Economic Development

Commercial property is taxed at up to 175% of the residential rate and generates revenue without adding school-age children. Brookline has dramatically underperformed its commercial development potential for decades. The CHCA project alone could yield ~$5.6M/year — roughly the entire FY27 Town override ask.

10-year potential $8–15M / yr
  • Commercial parcel inventory & fiscal impact modeling
  • Zoning reform for stalled high-value sites
  • PILOT program for non-profit landholders
  • Out-of-district SpEd revenue expansion
🏛️

Branch 2

Legislative Strategy

When METCO and non-resident students require special education, Brookline bears the full cost — ~$28,000/student/year — with zero reimbursement. With 144 non-resident SpEd students, that's ~$4M/year Brookline subsidizes for other municipalities. Newton, Wellesley, and Lexington face the exact same problem. Together, they have a compelling case at the State House.

Recovered costs potential $4–8M / yr
  • METCO SpEd unreimbursed cost quantification
  • Coalition building with Newton, Wellesley, Lexington
  • State House delegation engagement
  • Chapter 70, Prop 2½, and transfer tax options
📊

Branch 3

The 2030 OPEB Pivot

In 2030, Brookline fully funds its pension — a decade ahead of the state mandate. This frees ~$40M/year. The current policy is to redirect all of it to OPEB (retiree healthcare). The E&RSC explicitly questioned this. No one has built a rigorous model of the alternatives. We will.

Operating relief potential (FY31+) $10–20M / yr
  • GASB 74/75 actuarial liability baseline
  • Four $40M allocation scenarios modeled
  • Moody's Aaa rating sensitivity analysis
  • GIC healthcare reform savings model

Standing on the E&RSC's Shoulders

What the experts got right — and what they left open.

The Expenditures & Revenues Study Committee published its 162-page final report on March 13, 2026, after a year of intensive work. It correctly diagnosed the structural deficit, identified healthcare costs and salary steps as the primary cost drivers, and issued nine concrete conditions the Schools must meet to earn override support.

But the committee was constrained. Its mandate was to recommend an override amount for May 2026 — not design a 10-year fiscal reform. It explicitly noted areas it couldn't fully explore. Those are the gaps this project fills.

We do not oppose the override. We are building the structural plan that should accompany it — and that makes the one after it unnecessary.

The E&RSC's 9 School Conditions — a foundation we build on

1
Negotiate healthcare premium share down from 83% (comparable median: ~75–78%)
2
Create mechanism to manage wage expense growth toward revenue growth
3
Commission a middle school model and classroom consolidation study
4
Complete MUNIS shared financial system implementation by end of FY27
5
Verify all teaching assignments comply with contractual terms
6
Audit and reduce technology leases and ed-tech subscriptions
7
Require third-party school space users (7am–5:30pm) to pay fair-market rent
8
Launch facilities governance and youth athletics coordination pilot in FY27
9
Move the BEEP early education revolving fund toward full-cost break-even
